*A story about seasons, three chords, an old upright piano, and the God who grows things in His time.*

> "To every thing there is a season, and a time to every purpose under the heaven."
> — Ecclesiastes 3:1

## The boy who didn't count

I grew up in a home full of love. Three boys and one girl. I'm the middle of the three brothers, and our sister is the last born.

Home was safe. Primary school was a different country.

For the first time I had to interface with people of every kind, and not all of them were kind. I was bullied. I struggled to relate. Slowly my confidence cracked. My grades followed it down. Every exam came with anxiety and a quiet voice that sat right next to me in the exam room and whispered the same sentence every time:

*"You don't count, man. You just don't count."*

It followed me for years. But God was already doing something I couldn't see.

> "For the LORD seeth not as man seeth; for man looketh on the outward appearance, but the LORD looketh on the heart."
> — 1 Samuel 16:7

## The old upright piano

In my primary school there was a music room. In that music room stood an old upright piano, a little tired and a little out of tune.

The first time I sat at it, I played three notes and, without anyone teaching me, a melody came out. By ear. On the first sitting.

Music runs in my family. My uncles sing, they play guitar, and instruments were always around me. But I'd never had the chance to play one. So that old piano became my secret place. When the classroom told me I didn't count, the music room told me there was still hope for me.

Looking back, I think God hid a seed in that room.

> "For who hath despised the day of small things?"
> — Zechariah 4:10

## Three chords in the key of A

In high school I served in Scripture Union as a singer and worship leader, but I didn't know how to play the guitar.

When I was 14, my brother, who was serving in children's church at Watoto Church Ntinda, came home with a guitar. It was a classical guitar with steel strings and a very high action. Every chord cost my fingertips something.

He also brought home chord charts printed on paper. He was learning too, so we learned together.

In one week I could play in one key, the key of A, and with those three major chords I could play almost any song.

That guitar became a place where I found my confidence. Things I was never noticed for, I started to shine in. One day my literature teacher, who had noticed something musical in me, invited me to the staff room to play guitar and lead worship over lunch. The student who wasn't an outstanding academic performer was now leading his teachers in worship. They were amazed. Honestly, so was I. None of us knew I had a gift, including me.

> "I will praise thee; for I am fearfully and wonderfully made."
> — Psalm 139:14

## Infinity, and the search for something more

In that season some friends and I started a band called Infinity. We had big dreams. We partnered with radio stations like Bob FM, which has since shut down. We did concerts. We played at hotels and clubs. We were going to be the next big thing in the country.

It was a beautiful season and I learned a lot. But I wasn't fulfilled. Somewhere inside me, the eight-year-old boy who used to serve in children's church was missing something the stage couldn't give him: community, family, and the presence of God.

So at about 17, I went back to serving at Watoto Church seriously. They welcomed me with open arms. I found friends I hadn't seen in years. I found community. I found home.

## "I want you to play bass"

Fast forward to campus. The band trainer for Watoto at the time, a man we called TSP, called me into a room and said, "I'd really like you to serve in the adult church."

I was very comfortable in children's church.

Then he said, "And I want you to play bass."

I had never played bass in my life.

I was shy, and suddenly I was surrounded by so much talent it was intimidating. My first time playing electric guitar in the adult church was at a Cantata, a big production. I had never played at that level. And that old voice from primary school came back, word for word:

*"Man, you're not good. You'll never make it."*

In those moments I had to do what my namesake did:

> "But David encouraged himself in the LORD his God."
> — 1 Samuel 30:6

I had to remind myself, again and again, that I didn't call myself. God did.

> "Ye have not chosen me, but I have chosen you, and ordained you, that ye should go and bring forth fruit."
> — John 15:16

Years later, by God's grace, the boy who was terrified at his first Cantata was asked to take over from TSP and lead the musicians. I still can't fully explain it, except to say that it was God's doing.

## A seed can't water itself

Here's what I've come to understand about seasons.

A seed in the ground doesn't decide how fast it grows. It can't water itself. It can't send for sunshine. It can't hurry the harvest. All it can do is stay in the ground and trust the farmer who planted it.

> "I have planted, Apollos watered; but God gave the increase. So then neither is he that planteth any thing, neither he that watereth; but God that giveth the increase."
> — 1 Corinthians 3:6–7

Growth takes patience. It takes prayer. It takes trusting that the Farmer who put you in that ground will water you and let you grow in His time.

In my seasons I've learned patience: patience with God, patience with myself, and, the hardest one, patience with people. I'm still learning. God has also let me serve near leaders I once admired from far away, and every time I hear them speak I'm still amazed.

When I think about Watoto Church and the 40-plus years of history it carries, I'm humbled that God picked me to serve in that season, and that He's still using me in this one.

## To you, in your season

Maybe you're in the ground right now. It's dark, it's quiet, and nothing seems to be happening. Maybe you're impatient. Maybe you're asking whether God really called you to be where you are, or whether that old voice is right and you don't count.

Let me tell you what the music room told me: there is still hope for you.

> "And let us not be weary in well doing: for in due season we shall reap, if we faint not."
> — Galatians 6:9

> "He which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ."
> — Philippians 1:6

You count to God. You counted to Him before you played your first chord, passed your first exam, or got your first "well done." He proved it at the cross, where Jesus gave His life for you. Not for a better version of you. For you, as you are.

If you've never trusted Him with your life, today can be the day. You don't need three chords or a perfect report card. Jesus says:

> "Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest."
> — Matthew 11:28

If that's your heart, you can pray something simple like this:

*"Lord Jesus, I've been trying to grow on my own, and I'm tired. I believe You died and rose again for me. I give You my life, my past and my future. Plant me where You want me, water me, and grow me in Your time. Amen."*

If you prayed that, please don't keep it to yourself. Tell someone, find a local church, and let a community walk with you. That's what saved me when I was 17.

Stay in the ground. The Farmer knows what He's doing.

— David
